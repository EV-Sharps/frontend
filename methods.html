Why the correction isn't flat

Mult/Additive assume probability itself is the right ruler — a flat markup, everywhere. Probit assumes the vig lives in z-score space, where "how many standard deviations from the market's center" behaves linearly, and converts back through this curve. The curve is steep near a coinflip and flat out at the tails — so the same underlying mispricing reads as a small probability move near 50%, and a bigger one out where a homer prop lives.

At -110, the tangent line (the flat, linear approximation Mult/Additive effectively use) sits right on top of the actual curve — barely distinguishable. At +600, the curve has already bent well away from that same straight-line approximation. Probit reads the correction off the actual curve; Mult/Additive read it off the tangent — fine near the middle, increasingly wrong the further out you go.
