How to read these moves

Fair value removes the sportsbook margin by dividing each side's implied probability by the sum of all sides at the same book. Both sides are required (all three for a three-way market); missing prices are never invented.

Your window applies to both the play list and chart. The baseline uses the most recent available quote at the start of that window, or the first usable capture within it. An earlier quote carried into the window is marked on the chart; it is not a new observation. Windows are limited to today's Eastern-date history.

Sportsbook consensus compares at least two sportsbooks with actual paired prices at the baseline and latest usable capture. The same group is required throughout the fair chart; missing members create gaps. If there is no matched group, endpoint fair values are shown with their book coverage, without a consensus move. Select one reference book to see its fair value alone; a missing endpoint stays blank.

Movement is measured in percentage points, not the numerical difference between American prices. The chart's American-odds axis spaces prices by probability, so crossing even money does not create an artificial jump. On the probability scale, book lines show implied probability including margin; the fair line excludes margin.

History starts with the first observed pre-game capture today, not a sportsbook's official opening price. New captures are recorded about every 30 minutes. Each handicap is tracked separately; NHL anytime goalscorer includes only 0.5 (1+ goal). History resets at midnight Eastern.
